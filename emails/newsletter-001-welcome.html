We’re here to change that.

A Letter from our Founder

Matt McMahon

Founder & President, NCOMA
 

I started NCOMA because of a simple question that nobody in the US restaurant industry could answer: what is actually in your frying oil?

Not what brand. Not how old. What’s in it — the carcinogens, the aldehydes, the trans fats, the free radicals that form every time oil degrades past the point of safe use.

In Europe, the answer is regulated. Germany mandates discard at 27% Total Polar Materials. Belgium, Spain, and France set the line at 25%. Inspectors test. Kitchens comply. Diners are protected.

In the United States? Nothing. No federal threshold. No testing requirement. No standard at all. Over one million foodservice outlets, and not one is required by law to check the quality of the oil it serves food from.

NCOMA — the National Cooking Oil Management Association — exists to fill that gap. Not with regulation, but with a voluntary, science-based certification standard that any restaurant can adopt and any diner can verify.

This newsletter will bring you the science, the data, and the industry standards as we build them. Welcome to the oil transparency movement.

Matt McMahon

Why Oil Management Matters

Health. Economics. Food quality.

♡

It’s a health issue

Degraded frying oil produces aldehydes, trans fats, and free radicals linked to cardiovascular disease, liver damage, and cancer. The 24–27% TPM threshold isn’t arbitrary — it’s the point where oxidized triglyceride monomers reach levels shown to cause oxidative stress in animal studies. Your customers are eating whatever is in that fryer.

$

It’s an economics issue

Managed oil lasts 20–40% longer. Daily filtration alone extends life by 40–80%. A “cheap” sunflower oil at $1,262/ton lasting 107 fry cycles costs 3.9× more per cycle than premium high-oleic palm at $1,050/ton lasting 355 cycles. Most kitchens are bleeding money they can’t see.

★

It’s a food quality issue

Degraded oil increases fat absorption by 20–40%, producing greasier food with off-flavors from hexanal and short-chain aldehydes. Managed oil means crispier texture, cleaner taste, and less oil absorbed per portion. Better oil makes better food — and better food makes better business.
