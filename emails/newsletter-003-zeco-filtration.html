Your oil is only as good
as your filtration.
Most kitchens get this wrong.

In Issue #2, we showed you the science: a fruit oil like HOPO lasts 2–3× longer than any seed oil in the fryer. But even the best oil in the world degrades faster than it should if you’re not filtering it properly.

Here’s the uncomfortable truth: most commercial kitchens either don’t filter, filter wrong, or use equipment that lets food particles bypass the filter entirely. That means the oil you think is clean — isn’t.

This issue, we break down why filtration matters more than most operators realize — and introduce you to the inventor who patented the only sealed filtration system in the US.

The Problem

Why “we filter every day” doesn’t mean what you think.

Every time food hits hot oil, it sheds particles — breading, batter, protein fragments, carbonized crumbs. Those particles don’t just sit there. They accelerate oxidation, raise your oil’s Total Polar Compounds (TPC), darken the color, and produce off-flavors. The longer particles stay in your oil, the faster the oil dies.

Daily filtration can extend oil life by 40–80%. But only if the filtration actually works. And here’s where most kitchens fail:

How most kitchens “filter” today

Stock Pot + Strainer

Open pot of 350°F oil carried across the kitchen. Catches big crumbs only. Fine particles pass right through. Burn injuries, spills, liability.

✗

Cone / Coffee Filters

Gravity-fed. Slow. Oil cools during the process, which means reheating — which causes additional thermal degradation. Inconsistent micron rating.

✗

Built-In Fryer Filtration

Better than nothing — but no sealed path. Oil bypasses the filter around the edges. Fine micro-contaminants stay in the oil. Doesn’t wash the vat.

~

Every method above is “passive filtration” —
oil passes around the filter, not through it.

The result: micro-contaminants stay in your oil, accelerating breakdown.
